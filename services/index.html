Water problems come in different shapes, and each of our six services answers one of them. A pipe that just burst needs pumps, a wall that stays damp for days needs drying equipment, and a stained ceiling may need rebuilding once it is dry.

This page explains which service fits which situation so you can describe your problem clearly on the phone. If you are unsure, call (626) 879-0276 and dispatch will help you choose.

Choosing by situation

The simplest way to choose is to ask what is happening right now. If water is still on the floor, you need extraction first. If the water is gone but walls and floors feel damp, you need drying. If everything is dry and the damage is visible, you need rebuilding.

Emergency Water Removal

Best when water is standing on floors or carpet right now, such as after a burst supply line or a water heater failure.

Water Mitigation

Best in the first days, when moisture is soaking into drywall, subfloor and framing and needs controlled drying.

Water Damage Cleanup

Best when wet debris, soaked contents or dirty water must be removed and the room sanitized.

Flood Damage Restoration

Best after storm runoff, groundwater or sewage backup, where the water is contaminated.

Water Damage Repair

Best once materials are dry and drywall, paint and flooring must be replaced.

Mold Remediation

Best when moisture has already produced visible or suspected mold growth.

How the services usually combine

Real jobs rarely use only one service. A kitchen leak might begin with emergency water removal, continue with water mitigation for several days of drying, and finish with water damage repair once readings are normal.

Sewage and storm water change the order. Contaminated water means porous materials like carpet pad and low drywall usually come out early, which is the core of flood damage restoration. Removal and sanitizing are then handled under water damage cleanup before any rebuilding starts.

Mold is the one service we try to avoid needing. It follows moisture, so mold remediation begins only after the leak is repaired and the area is being dried. Fast drying in the first 24 to 48 hours, as EPA guidance recommends, is the best way to avoid it.

What happens on a typical job

The crew starts by finding the water source and checking where it has traveled. Moisture meters read the wood and drywall, and a thermal imaging camera shows cool, wet areas behind paint that look dry to the eye. Those readings set the plan and are recorded so you can compare them later.

Extraction comes next with truck-mounted or portable units, followed by air movers placed to push air across wet surfaces and LGR dehumidifiers to pull moisture out of the air. Drying usually runs for several days, with readings checked on each visit. Fans and dehumidifiers are pulled once the readings on wood and drywall match dry reference points.

Our crews can work to the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage and S520 for mold, and you can ask any company you are considering whether its crews do. These are industry documents, not a promise about any one job.

What we do not do

Restoration is not waterproofing or foundation repair, and we are not a plumbing company. If a pipe must be replaced or a roof must be patched, a plumber or roofer fixes the source while we handle the water damage. Fixing the source first matters, because drying a room that keeps getting wet only wastes time.

We also do not guess at prices over the phone. Before any work begins, you get a scope and a price built on the water category, the area hit, the materials involved and the expected drying days.
