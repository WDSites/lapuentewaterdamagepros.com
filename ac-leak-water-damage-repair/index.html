Why air conditioners leak inside a house

Cooling air pulls moisture out of the air, and that condensation drips off the evaporator coil into a pan and out through a drain line. When the line clogs with slime or debris, the pan fills and overflows. A cracked pan, a rusted pan in an older unit or a disconnected drain fitting gives the same result.

Cooling runs hard through Southern California summers, so a system that is overloaded with condensate can overflow on the first stretch of hot weather and keep going every day. An air handler in the attic is the classic trouble spot, because the water falls on insulation and drywall and nobody sees it until a ceiling stain shows up. Ductless wall units can also drip down a wall if their drain tube kinks or the unit is not level.

The city describes La Puente as primarily residential, about 70 percent of its land, so most calls like this come from houses, duplexes and townhomes with attic or closet air handlers. If yours sits in an attic, a quick look at the drain pan and the ceiling below it every spring is worth the minute.

First safety steps for an AC leak

Power, water and ceilings

An air handler is wired to a breaker and often sits above a ceiling that is now holding water.

  • Switch the system off at the thermostat, then at its breaker or service switch only if the panel is dry and easy to reach.
  • If water is dripping from a light fixture, stay out of the room and turn off that circuit only when the panel is dry. Otherwise call an electrician.
  • A burning smell, sparks or a gas odor near a furnace means leave and call 911 or the gas utility first.
  • Do not stand under a bulging ceiling. It can release a lot of water suddenly.
  • Place a bucket under the drip and set a towel around it. Do not climb into a hot attic alone.

How we dry and repair AC water damage

The first visit uses a moisture meter on the ceiling, walls and baseboards below the unit, then a thermal imaging camera to find the edges of the wet area. If the air handler is in the attic, the crew checks the insulation around and under it, because wet batts hold moisture against the drywall.

Wet insulation that has matted down is removed. Portable extraction pulls water from the floor, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the cavity and the drywall. Where a ceiling has sagged or the paper has softened, the section is cut out above the wet line. A HEPA air scrubber runs if dust or old insulation is disturbed.

Condensate water usually starts clean, but a drain line holds algae and slime, so it should not be treated as pure drinking water. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published water damage standard, and you can ask any contractor whether its crews work to it. After drying, drywall water damage repair or water damage repair covers patching, texture and paint.

We are not HVAC technicians, plumbers or electricians, and we do not do waterproofing or foundation repair. A licensed HVAC contractor clears the drain line, replaces a cracked pan and checks the coil. The California Contractors State License Board says a contractor with employees must hold a valid license regardless of job size, so ask to see the license of whoever you hire.

When the stain is from the AC and not the roof

A ceiling stain can come from a roof, a pipe or an air conditioner, and the right fix depends on which. An AC stain tends to appear after the system has been running and near the unit or a duct, while a roof stain grows after rain. If you are not sure, write down when it grows and tell the dispatcher.

Insurance and AC leak claims

The California Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood but do cover other kinds of water damage, and it warns that a policy may not cover damage that follows an ignored problem. A sudden pan failure is often treated differently from a drain that has dripped for months, so check your policy and ask your insurer how it classifies yours.

The department advises photographing damage, keeping receipts and getting itemized bids from licensed contractors. Keep the HVAC technician's invoice, which shows the cause. The repair of the unit itself may not be covered even when the resulting damage is, and we cannot predict any outcome.

Mistakes to avoid and how to prevent AC leaks

  • Running the system after you see a stain, which keeps making condensate.
  • Repainting before the ceiling and insulation are dry.
  • Pouring bleach into a drain line without asking an HVAC technician.
  • Ignoring a musty smell. EPA guidance says that if mold is growing you must clean it up and fix the water problem.

To prevent overflow, have the drain line flushed and the pan checked before cooling season, change filters on schedule and consider a float switch that shuts the unit down when the pan fills. If mold has already formed, mold remediation follows once the moisture is fixed.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency AC leak water damage service available in La Puente?

Yes. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and same-day response is offered when a crew is available. No arrival time is guaranteed, so switch the system off and protect the floor while you wait.

Why is my AC leaking water inside the house?

Most often the condensate drain line is clogged and the pan overflows. A cracked pan, a loose drain fitting or a frozen coil that melts can also do it. An HVAC technician finds the cause.

Can you fix the air conditioner?

No. We dry and repair the damage. A licensed HVAC contractor repairs the unit, clears the line and replaces parts.

Is it safe to keep running the AC with a small leak?

It is better to switch it off until the drain is cleared, since every hour of running adds water. Use fans for comfort if you can.

The ceiling stain is dry now. Is it done?

A dry surface does not mean the insulation and framing above are dry. A moisture reading shows whether anything is still wet before you paint.

How long does drying take?

A small ceiling and insulation area often takes a few days, and larger or longer-running leaks take more. The crew checks readings on each visit and stops when materials match a dry reference.
