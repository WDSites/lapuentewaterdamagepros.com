Winter storms and hillside runoff in the Puente Hills

The county community plan flags slopes steeper than 25 percent across the southern part of Hacienda Heights and along its western hills, which are part of the Puente Hills. The same plan says development in landslide and flood risk areas is to be restricted. For a homeowner, that means a hillside lot can send runoff toward a garage, a side yard or a downslope wall, so check where the ground drains before the rain starts.

Three homes near Gotera Drive were evacuated in 2024 after a hillside gave way, and press accounts describe a closed stretch of Hacienda Road where the storm had undercut the pavement. A heavy storm can move soil and water in ways a gutter cannot handle. If mud or runoff is heading for your home, keep your distance and call 911 if anyone is at risk before you call us.

Storm water that enters a home is Category 2 or 3 under the standard IICRC water categories. It has crossed soil, yards and driveways, so it can carry silt, fuel residue and bacteria. Carpet, pad and wet drywall that touched it are usually removed rather than dried in place, and the cleanup follows the practices in flood damage restoration.

When water is spreading in Hacienda Heights

Water on the floor is an emergency when it keeps coming, reaches outlets, or sits in a room people use. Stop the source first. If a pipe or fixture is the culprit, the main water valve is typically on the street side of the house or against the garage wall.

Power is the next worry. Wiring or a gas smell near the water means staying out and calling 911 or the utility first. After that, dial (626) 879-0276 and report what is wet, how far it reaches and whether the water looks clean or murky.

Night calls are dispatched exactly like daytime ones. Once a crew arrives, emergency water removal begins with extraction, using truck-mounted or portable units to lift standing water ahead of any drying.

Dry-season causes inside the walls

Not every loss in Hacienda Heights comes from a storm. The county plan describes homes built from the 1950s onward, with early subdivisions around Kwis Avenue, so many houses have decades-old supply lines, water heaters and shutoff valves. An aging water heater tank that lets go can put a few dozen gallons across a hallway in minutes.

The usual dry-season culprits are:

  • Supply lines and braided connectors under sinks and behind toilets that crack or weep for weeks before anyone notices.
  • Water heaters in garages and closets, where a corroded tank or failed relief valve drains into the slab edge and drywall.
  • Irrigation lines and sprinkler heads set close to a foundation, which can soak stucco and sill plates.
  • AC condensate lines that clog and overflow into an attic or a ceiling below the air handler.

Before We Arrive

Before We Arrive

Hillside homes add a few wrinkles, so these four steps lean toward runoff and slick ground.

Stop the source

Close the main or the fixture valve. If storm water is the source, clear leaves from the nearest drain and steer runoff from the door, but only where the ground is stable.

Power off wet areas

Leave breakers alone if the panel is damp or the garage floor is wet around it. A dry, reachable panel is the only case for switching off wet rooms, and 911 covers the rest.

Lift what you can

Furniture legs go on foil or blocks, rugs come up, and electronics, papers and photos move to a dry room upslope or upstairs.

Keep people and pets out

Runoff and sewage carry contamination, so children and animals stay back. The line at (626) 879-0276 is staffed all night.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Hacienda Heights?

Hacienda Heights is served under ZIP 91745, and we cover it fully. Rowland Heights (91748) adjoins the community to the east and is a separate place. La Puente ZIPs 91744 and 91746 sit to the north, and our crews are based there. An address outside that list still deserves a call to (626) 879-0276, since coverage can be settled by phone.

Is emergency water removal available in Hacienda Heights?

Yes, around the clock. Ring (626) 879-0276, say what is wet, and a crew is arranged, often the same day. Pumps come first (truck-mounted or portable), and drying gear follows.

Is storm runoff in my home considered contaminated?

Yes, it is treated as Category 2 or 3 water, because it crosses soil and yards. Porous materials that it touched, such as carpet pad and low drywall, are usually removed and replaced rather than dried.

Will insurance pay for storm or flood water in my house?

It depends on the cause and the policy. A burst heater tank and rising runoff are treated differently. The first is commonly covered, while the second generally falls to a separate NFIP or private flood policy. Your agent can read the wording with you.

Who handles building permits in Hacienda Heights?

Hacienda Heights is unincorporated, so the county, not a city, is the authority. Ask the county before starting rebuild work that may need a permit.

How long does drying usually take?

Three to five days is common with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, though category, materials and room size shift that. Readings from moisture meters, not the calendar, mark the finish line.

How do I know whether I have a hidden leak?

Watch for stains, a musty smell, peeling paint or a rising water bill. Pinpointing the wet spot without opening the wall takes moisture meters plus an infrared camera.
