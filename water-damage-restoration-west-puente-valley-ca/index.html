Where West Puente Valley sits and what Bassett means

The county's area plan describes about 1.9 square miles of single-family subdivisions named for Rancho La Puente, bordering Baldwin Park and West Covina to the north, with Bassett Park and Allen J. Martin Park as its parks. Some neighbors say Bassett for this same area, so tell dispatch whichever name you use and add your street.

Addresses here commonly carry the La Puente ZIP codes 91744 or 91746, so check yours on your tax bill rather than assuming. A wet floor is handled the same way whichever jurisdiction the house falls under.

What the housing numbers tell a homeowner

Census QuickFacts show a 2020 population of 22,959 and an owner-occupied housing rate of 77.8 percent for 2020 to 2024. With most households owning, the owner usually decides how fast to act, so shut off the supply and call early. Common indoor sources include supply lines, appliance hoses, a water heater and a ceiling.

The same figures list 4.34 persons per household. More people means more laundry and showers, a reason to inspect washer hoses and under-sink valves, not a statement about how often leaks happen. Renters should photograph the damage and tell the landlord the same day.

Creek channels and heavy rain: what to check

The county plan says the Walnut Creek and Puente Creek Channels both run through the community. A channel nearby says nothing about any single house, so look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask the county, because neighborhood-specific flood-zone status is not verified here.

The city of La Puente's 2024 draft Safety Element includes a Santa Fe Dam inundation scenario that reaches the northwest corner of this area. It is a planning analysis, not a forecast or a designation of any home, so keep insurance papers and photos where you can grab them.

Storm water that enters through a door or garage has crossed yards and pavement, so it is treated as Category 2 or 3. Carpet, pad and soaked drywall it touched are usually removed, as described under flood damage restoration.

First-hour safety steps

Before you touch anything

Your safety matters more than the carpet, so work from dry ground.

  • Smell gas or see sparks? Leave and call 911 or the gas utility first, then call us.
  • Shut off the main water valve only if you can reach it without stepping into water.
  • Never touch switches, outlets or the breaker panel while standing in water.
  • Keep people and pets out of sewage or storm water, and do not wade in to rescue belongings.
  • Photograph from a dry spot, then call (626) 879-0276.

How extraction and drying work

We start with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera to find water behind walls and under flooring. Extraction units remove standing water, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the structure. EPA guidance says to dry within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold, and ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference.

Not everything can be saved. Particleboard, saturated insulation and anything touched by sewage often has to go. We do not repair plumbing, roofs or electrical systems, so a plumber, roofer or electrician fixes the source.

Documenting damage for your insurance claim

The California Department of Insurance advises photographing damage and keeping receipts, and says coverage depends on the cause and your policy terms. Take wide and close photos before cleanup and ask your insurer how it treats a sudden pipe failure versus storm water.

We provide moisture readings and an itemized scope so your paperwork matches the work. Coverage and approval are your insurer's decisions, and we cannot promise either.
