Structural Drying Services in La Puente, CA: What Happens Inside Walls and Floors

The equipment, the measurements and the daily checks behind a dry house.

By the La Puente Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

Call (626) 879-0276

Structural drying services remove water from the parts of a house you cannot see, like wall cavities, subfloors, framing and the layers under flooring. Mopping and a box fan dry the surface, but the lumber, insulation and drywall behind it can stay wet for weeks.

This guide explains what the equipment does, how crews map moisture, and how they decide a building is actually dry. No one can honestly promise a day count before measuring, so you will see what drives the timeline instead.

What structural drying means

Structural drying is the controlled removal of moisture from building materials after a water loss. It follows the first response, where standing water is extracted and the source is stopped. The work happens in the walls, floors and ceilings, and it continues until readings match what a normal dry home looks like.

Drying goes best when it begins inside the EPA's 24 to 48 hour mold-prevention window, and crews then track readings until the framing hits its target. Surface-dry is not the finish line. Wood framing, concrete slabs and thick insulation release water slowly into the air, and the equipment has to keep up with that release.

Moisture mapping: finding where the water went

The first job on site is a walk-through with meters and an infrared camera, and no air mover goes down until the wet zones are mapped. The camera shows temperature differences that often trace wet areas behind paint, and the meter confirms the number. The result is a moisture map, a drawing or log of each room with readings at fixed points.

Water travels. It wicks up drywall, runs along joists and pools under cabinets, so a leak in a kitchen can leave wet material in the next room. A good map includes areas that look dry so there is a baseline and nothing is missed.

Why a dry reference reading matters

Materials differ, so a single magic number does not exist. Crews take a reading from an unaffected wall, floor or stud of the same material and use it as the dry goal. That keeps the drying target honest for your house instead of a generic chart.

The equipment and what each piece does

Air movers

High-velocity fans that push air across wet surfaces so water evaporates into the room. Placement matters more than quantity, and they are angled along walls and under cabinets.

LGR dehumidifiers

Low grain refrigerant units that pull evaporated moisture out of the air. Without them the water just recirculates, and the room feels like a sauna.

Injection and mat systems

Hoses and drying mats move dry air into wall cavities or through a wood floor so material dries from the inside.

Extraction and air scrubbers

Truck-mounted or portable extraction units remove bulk water, while HEPA air scrubbers clean the air if drywall or insulation has been opened.

Closed doors and windows matter because the dehumidifier works best when the room is a sealed loop. Open windows let humid outdoor air in, and the machines struggle against it. The crew will explain which zones to keep closed.

If the water was contaminated or large in volume, drying begins after emergency water removal has pulled out standing water and any unsalvageable materials.

Typical drying monitoring, step by step

Drying is a process, not a single visit. The crew sets equipment, returns on a schedule to read the meters, changes equipment placement and removes units as zones reach goal. The table shows the usual rhythm, though real timelines depend on the water category, the materials and the weather.

Many homes need several days of drying, and larger or contaminated losses can take longer. Anyone quoting an exact number before measuring is guessing. Ask for the daily logs, because they are what you will hand to an insurer.

Drying and prevention overlap with water mitigation, which covers the first 24 to 72 hours of limiting the spread, while structural drying is the sustained follow-through.

Signs the drying is not finished

  • A musty smell comes back after the equipment is removed.
  • Baseboards, cabinet kicks or flooring seams show new swelling or discoloration.
  • Paint bubbles or wallpaper edges lift on a wall that was wet.
  • Windows fog or condensation appears on cool surfaces inside.
  • A moisture meter still reads higher than the dry reference reading.

If any of those show up, call the crew and ask for another reading. Drying that stops too early can leave pockets of moisture in framing. If mold appears, the response moves into mold remediation, and S520 is the published industry standard for that work.

What you can do during drying

  1. Keep the equipment running and do not turn it off to save noise, because a stop restarts the clock.
  2. Keep exterior doors and windows closed in the drying zones unless the crew says otherwise.
  3. Do not move or unplug machines. Ask the crew if a cord is in the way.
  4. Keep children and pets away from the equipment and the open wall cavities.
  5. Photograph the setup and ask for copies of the moisture logs.
  6. Do not paint, carpet or cover anything until readings are signed off.

Safety while drying equipment runs

Drying equipment is powerful and runs around the clock.

  • Do not unplug, move or switch off air movers or dehumidifiers on your own.
  • Keep cords out of any standing water and do not add extension cords.
  • Keep children and pets away from the equipment and the wet area.
  • Do not close off drying cavities or seal vents without asking your technician.
  • Call (626) 879-0276 if equipment trips a breaker, smells hot or makes a new noise.

Frequently asked questions

How long does structural drying take?

Duration depends on water category, materials and loss size, which is why crews measure it day by day instead of quoting a fixed number. Crews keep equipment running and take daily readings until the materials match a dry reference reading.

What is the difference between water extraction and structural drying?

Extraction removes standing and bulk water, usually with truck-mounted or portable units. Structural drying removes the moisture left in framing, subfloors and drywall using air movers and dehumidifiers.

Can I use box fans and a home dehumidifier instead?

They help with small, clean-water spills, but they rarely dry wall cavities or subfloors. Without moisture readings you cannot tell when drying is done, which is how hidden mold starts.

Will my power bill go up during drying?

Air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, so electricity use rises while they operate. Ask your insurer how that is handled, and ask the crew how many machines are planned.

Do older Baldwin Park and Hacienda Heights homes dry differently?

Material and construction type matter, so older homes can behave differently from newer ones, and moisture mapping shows the difference. See Baldwin Park water damage restoration or Hacienda Heights water damage restoration for local coverage.
