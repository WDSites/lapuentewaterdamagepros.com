Does Insurance Cover Water Damage in La Puente, CA?

What homeowners policies commonly cover, what they commonly exclude, and why your own policy decides.

By the La Puente Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

Call (626) 879-0276

Does insurance cover water damage? Often yes when the water was sudden and accidental, and often no when it was a slow leak or a flood. In the words of the state insurance department, a homeowners policy leaves flood damage out and picks up most other water damage.

Policies differ, so this page describes common patterns only. Read your own policy, and ask your insurer before assuming anything about a claim.

Sudden and accidental versus gradual

Most homeowners policies draw a line between water that arrives suddenly and water that seeps over time. A supply line that snaps, a water heater that ruptures or a washing machine hose that splits is the sudden kind. A drip under a sink that ran for months is the gradual kind.

The distinction matters because insurers often treat gradual leaks as maintenance. The CDI also warns homeowners never to ignore signs of an obvious water problem and notes that a policy may not cover ensuing damage. Repairing a small leak early protects both the house and any later claim.

The cause of the loss usually decides how it is handled, not where the wet spot appears. A stained ceiling can come from a burst pipe above (often covered) or a roof that has leaked for years (often not). Your adjuster looks at the source.

Flood damage and the NFIP

Standard homeowners policies exclude flood, along with earth movement, earthquake, landslide and mudflow, according to the CDI. Flood coverage is a separate product. The federal National Flood Insurance Program, administered by FEMA, is one source, and private flood policies exist too.

The CDI notes that it does not regulate the NFIP, so questions about those policies go to the program or the agent who sold it. Waiting periods often apply to new flood policies, so buying coverage on the day before a storm is not a plan. What to do right after the loss

The CDI tells owners to stop further loss where they can and to hold on to receipts for sensible protective spending. Take photos first, before anything is moved or thrown out, and leave permanent repairs until the adjuster has seen the damage. Insurers generally expect a reasonable effort to limit the loss.

  1. Stop the water source and shut off the main valve if you can do so safely.
  2. Photograph and video every wet area and every damaged item.
  3. Call your insurer or agent to report the loss and ask what they need.
  4. Start drying or water mitigation so the damage does not spread.
  5. Keep receipts for emergency services, hotel stays and supplies.
  6. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has approved discarding them.

Claim timelines in California

California regulations set deadlines for insurers. The clock runs like this: 15 calendar days to acknowledge your notice, 40 calendar days after proof of claim to accept or deny, then 30 days to pay once accepted. If more time is needed, the insurer must send a written notice about every 30 days.

Those timelines come from the Department's published materials, and your situation may differ. If a claim stalls, the CDI offers consumer help. A claim log with dates, names and call summaries makes any follow-up easier.

Documentation checklist

  • Dated photos and video of all damage, taken before cleanup
  • Policy number and the insurer's claim line saved in your phone
  • Moisture readings and equipment logs from your restoration crew
  • Receipts for protection costs and temporary housing
  • Written itemized bids from licensed contractors
  • Call notes: who you reached, when, and what they said

You can also ask a restoration firm if its technicians follow ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry's published water damage standard. Insurance questions for La Puente homeowners

The CDI guidance states that insurers must follow set deadlines, which means the clock starts when you report. Report water damage promptly and ask for the claim number, so you can track the 15 day acknowledgment window against your own calendar.

The 2024 draft Safety Element for La Puente puts the city outside the 100 and 500 year flood zones, with the 500 year zone only brushing the northeast edge. It also names localized roadway flooding in significant storms. A favorable flood zone rating does not alter the exclusion in a homeowners policy, so look at the FEMA map for your street and ask whether stand-alone flood coverage makes sense for you.

Los Angeles County asks residents to find sandbag locations, sign up for Alert LA County and note that flood insurance is a separate NFIP policy. If you rent your home, ask your landlord about the building policy and ask your own agent about contents coverage.

Frequently asked questions

Is water damage from a burst pipe covered by homeowners insurance?

Often yes, because a burst pipe is typically sudden and accidental. Policies differ on whether the pipe repair itself is covered, so ask your insurer. Document the loss and start drying promptly.

Does homeowners insurance cover a slow leak?

Often not. Many policies exclude gradual seepage and treat it as maintenance. Fixing small leaks early and keeping records of repairs can help you understand your options.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Standard policies exclude flood, according to the California Department of Insurance. A separate flood policy, such as one through the NFIP, is needed. Ask your agent which coverage applies to your address.

How long does an insurer have to respond to a claim in California?

State regulations set a 15 calendar day acknowledgment, a 40 calendar day decision after proof of claim and a 30 day payment window after acceptance. Extensions require written notice. Confirm current rules with the Department of Insurance.

Should my La Puente home carry flood insurance if it is not in a flood zone?

That is a personal decision to discuss with your agent. The draft city safety plan notes localized roadway flooding in strong storms, and the homeowners exclusion still applies. Your own street's FEMA map entry is the place to start.
