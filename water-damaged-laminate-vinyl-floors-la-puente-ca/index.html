Water Damaged Laminate and Vinyl Floors in La Puente, CA: What Dries and What Goes

A material-by-material look at floors that float, click and swell.

By the La Puente Water Damage Pros team · Published October 27, 2026

Call (626) 879-0276

Water damaged laminate and vinyl floors behave very differently, even though they look alike from the doorway. Laminate has a wood-fiber core that swells and does not shrink back, while vinyl plank is mostly plastic and can survive a soaking on its own.

The real problem is often what lies underneath, since water slides through the seams and sits in the subfloor. This guide shows what each material does, what can dry, what usually has to be replaced, and how to check before you lay anything new.

Laminate versus vinyl plank: why the same leak ends differently

Laminate is a photo layer on top of a core made of compressed wood fiber. When water reaches that core through the seams, it swells at the edges, the planks peak or lift and the surface looks chewed. The fibers do not return to their original shape, so swollen laminate normally gets replaced.

Luxury vinyl plank, often called LVP, is made of layered plastic and does not absorb water the same way. The planks themselves may survive, but water can run under them and collect on the subfloor. Rigid-core products behave better than thin glue-down sheets, and edges curling or a musty smell tells you moisture is trapped.

Both floors are usually installed floating, meaning they sit over an underlayment instead of being nailed down. That helps if you need to lift them to dry, and it hurts because the underlayment holds water like a sponge.

A floor that looks fine can still hide a wet subfloor. If you intend to keep vinyl plank, lift a few boards and check what is underneath before you decide.

How to check before you trust a floor

Look first, then measure. Run a hand across the seams for raised edges, look for dark staining or bubbling, and sniff for a musty smell near baseboards. Gaps that open or close with your weight are another clue, since floating floors should feel solid underfoot.

A moisture meter on the subfloor and a thermal imaging camera on the surface give you real numbers. Crews compare readings to an unaffected area of the same material to decide whether it is actually dry. If you rely only on a surface that feels dry, you risk trapping moisture under a new floor.

Why 24 to 48 hours matters

Floating planks and vinyl backing trap damp underneath, so the EPA's 24 to 48 hour mark for stopping mold applies fast here. Under planks and pad, that window can pass before you ever see a stain. A damp subfloor under a sealed floor is a quiet source of mold, so lift and check early.

Steps to take after a leak under laminate or vinyl

Stop the water

Close the supply valve and isolate the leak. Breakers for flooded rooms come off last, and only if the panel stays out of the water.

Remove standing water

Small spills can be handled with towels, a mop or a wet vac, while a deeper pool needs an extraction unit. Lift furniture and rugs off the planks.

Lift and open

Pull baseboards and a few planks near the wet area so air reaches the underlayment and subfloor. Take photos before and after.

Dry and measure

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, and a moisture meter confirms it before any new flooring goes down.

If water came from a failed pipe or appliance, the first days are covered by water mitigation, which limits how far moisture travels. For flooded rooms, contaminated water or sewage, flood damage restoration is the matching service.

Checklist: deciding whether to replace

  • Planks are swollen, peaked or crumbling at the edges.
  • The underlayment or foam pad is wet, stained or smells musty.
  • The subfloor reads wetter than a dry reference area.
  • The water source was a sewer backup, a storm or other contaminated water.
  • Boards will not re-lock after being lifted.
  • Staining, dark spots or mold growth appears under the planks.

If you answer yes to the first few, expect replacement. If the planks are clean, rigid and only the subfloor was damp, drying and reinstalling can be an option for vinyl plank. A technician can tell you with readings, not guesses.

Frequently asked questions

Can water damaged laminate flooring be saved?

Sometimes, if only the surface was wet and it was dried within hours. Once water reaches the fiber core and the edges swell, the planks rarely return to shape and usually need replacing.

Can you dry vinyl plank flooring after a leak?

Often yes, especially rigid-core planks exposed to clean water. Lift the planks, dry the subfloor and confirm it with a moisture meter before reinstalling.

How do I know if there is mold under my laminate or vinyl floor?

A musty smell, dark spots at the seams or planks that will not lie flat can point to trapped moisture. Lift a few planks to look, and ask a professional to test the subfloor with a moisture meter.

Is laminate or vinyl better in a home that may get wet?

Rigid-core vinyl plank generally tolerates water better than laminate, though neither fixes a leak. Either way, dry the subfloor before installing.

Do entry and garage floors in La Puente need extra attention after storms?

They can, since the city's draft Safety Element mentions localized roadway flooding in significant storms. Lift mats, check the entry seam for moisture and call if any plank edges look raised.
