Emergency Water Damage Cleanup in La Puente, CA

Get the water, the debris and the contamination out of the house, then clean what stays.

Call (626) 879-0276

Water damage cleanup in La Puente often begins with a bad moment, such as a toilet overflow, a washing machine flood or sewage in the tub, and our dispatch line answers 24/7. Until help arrives, close the main valve or stop the leak, get valuables and paper goods up off the floor, and keep children and pets away from water of unknown quality. Shutting off power to wet rooms is for dry panels and safe footing only.

Cleanup means removing the water, the debris and the materials that cannot be saved, then cleaning and sanitizing what remains. It comes before drying, and doing it in the right order protects everything else in the house.

When the water is dirty: sewage and contaminated flooding

Dirty water changes the job from a cleanup into a controlled removal. Sewage, toilet backups with waste, and floodwater that crossed a yard or street carry bacteria that soak into anything porous. Do not wade through it, do not run a household vacuum on it, and do not use bleach on carpet in the hope of saving it.

Before you touch anything

Contaminated water and electricity create two hazards at once, so slow down at the door.

  • Water over outlet height or lapping at the panel means do not go in, and sparks or a gas smell mean a call to 911 or the utility.
  • Switch off the breaker only when the panel is dry and you can stand on dry ground.
  • Wear rubber boots and gloves if you must enter, and wash with soap afterward.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with a medical condition away from the affected area.

Same-day service is available when crews are free, and nights and weekends are handled like any other call. Sewage never waits for office hours, so tell the dispatcher what came up, which rooms it reached and how long it has been sitting.

Category 1, 2 and 3 water under IICRC S500

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published industry standard for water damage restoration, and it sorts water into three categories by source. The category tells the crew how much protective gear, which cleaning products and how much material removal to plan for. A fair question for any contractor is whether its technicians follow S500 and which category it would assign to your water.

Water categories at a glance
CategoryTypical sourceWhat cleanup usually involves
1Broken supply line, sink overflow with clean waterExtraction, then drying, with limited cleaning if caught early
2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, aquarium, some toilet overflow without solidsExtraction, cleaning of surfaces, removal of porous pad and soft goods
3Sewage, river or creek water, rising ground water, long-standing stagnant waterContainment, removal of porous materials, full cleaning and sanitizing

Category is not fixed. Clean water that sits for days, warms up and soaks into carpet can slide into a higher category, which is one more reason to call early instead of waiting to see if it dries.

Removing the water and the debris

Extraction comes first

Standing water is pulled out with portable extraction units for rooms and truck-mounted units when volume is large. Carpet and pad are extracted before they are lifted, which reduces what drips through the house on the way out.

Then the tear-out

Crews cut out wet material along clean lines, and a moisture meter confirms where the wetness stops so nothing is removed without reason. For contaminated water, drywall and baseboard are cut above the visible line to leave a margin. Debris is bagged or contained before it is carried out, so dirty water is not tracked across clean floors.

What usually goes in the dumpster

  • Carpet pad and, after Category 3 exposure, the carpet itself
  • Drywall and insulation that wicked up contaminated water
  • Particleboard cabinet bases and laminate that swelled
  • Mattresses, upholstered furniture and stuffed toys that sat in dirty water
  • Cardboard storage boxes and loose paper

Clean-water items are judged one at a time instead of tossed by default. Solid wood furniture, tile and many hard-surface goods can be cleaned and kept, and a list with photos before disposal helps both your records and the crew.

Sanitizing what stays

Once the debris is gone, hard surfaces get a detergent wash to lift soil, because disinfectant does not work well on dirty surfaces. An antimicrobial is then applied and left on the surface for its full contact time. Framing that stays is wiped, scrubbed or sanded as needed.

Fine dust from demolition is the next concern. HEPA vacuums pick up what a normal vacuum would blow around, and HEPA air scrubbers clean the room air. For sewage jobs the crew often hangs plastic sheeting and runs negative air containment, which pulls air toward the work zone so it does not drift into clean rooms.

Drying follows, and it is covered on the water mitigation page. If any wet material was missed, mold becomes the risk, and mold remediation is a different job with its own S520 standard.

Contents: keep, clean or discard

Hard goods

Glass, metal, plastic and sealed wood can usually be washed and sanitized, even after dirty water.

Soft goods

Clothing and bedding from clean water often wash. After sewage, upholstery and mattresses are usually discarded.

Papers and electronics

Photos and documents can be frozen and dried by a specialist. Wet electronics should stay unplugged until someone has checked them.

What affects the cost of cleanup

  • Water category, since Category 3 needs containment and protective gear
  • Affected square footage and how many rooms the water reached
  • Materials to be removed and the volume of debris hauled
  • Cleaning scope, including contents and repeat treatments
  • Rebuild scope that follows once the structure is clean and dry

A homeowners policy often responds to an overflow or break that happens suddenly, and often does not respond to damage from long neglect or slow leaks. Flood damage is usually excluded, and the federal NFIP or a private flood policy is what covers it. Check your policy, ask your insurer what documentation it needs, and never assume an outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle water damage cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in La Puente?

Yes. Sewage backups and overflows do not follow a schedule, so the line takes night, weekend and holiday calls. If a crew is free you may be served the same day. No set arrival time is promised, but the dispatcher gives safe steps for the wait.

What is the difference between Category 1, 2 and 3 water?

Category 1 comes from a clean source such as a supply line. Category 2 carries some contamination, such as washing machine discharge. Category 3 is grossly contaminated, such as sewage or floodwater, and it needs containment and removal of porous materials.

Is it safe to clean up sewage water myself?

It is not recommended. Sewage contains bacteria and sits in porous materials where household cleaners will not reach. A crew with protective gear, extraction units and containment can do it with less risk to your family.

How do you sanitize a home after dirty water?

Surfaces are washed with detergent first, then treated with an antimicrobial left on for its contact time. HEPA vacuums and air scrubbers handle dust, and framing that stays is cleaned or sanded. The steps are done before drying equipment is set.

Can wet carpet be saved?

Carpet that got clean water and is caught within a day or two can often be lifted, cleaned and dried, though the pad is usually replaced. Carpet exposed to sewage or floodwater is generally discarded.

How long does cleanup take?

Extraction and tear-out for one or two rooms commonly take part of a day, and larger or contaminated jobs take longer. Drying then adds several more days. A moisture meter, not the calendar, decides when each phase ends.

Will my insurance pay for cleanup?

It may, when the damage was sudden and accidental, but gradual leaks, neglect and flood are often excluded. Photos and itemized receipts support whatever you decide to file, so keep both and ask your insurer what it needs. We cannot promise a claim outcome.
